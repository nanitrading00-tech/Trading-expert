If you have never placed a trade before, the process can look complicated. It is not. Here is the order most new investors should follow.

## 1. Open a demat and trading account

Choose a SEBI-registered broker and complete the KYC process with your PAN, Aadhaar and bank details. The account is usually ready within a day or two.

## 2. Understand the segments

| Segment | What you trade | Suited for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equity cash | Shares of a company | Beginners |
| Futures | Contracts on a stock or index | Experienced traders |
| Options | The right to buy or sell at a price | Experienced traders |
| Commodity | Gold, silver, crude and more | Diversification |

Start with equity cash. Move to derivatives only once you understand how leverage increases both profit and loss.

## 3. Decide how much you can risk

A common rule is to risk only a small part of your capital on any single trade. Decide that number before you place the order, not after the market moves.

## 4. Place your first trade with a stop-loss

Every order should have two levels fixed in advance: the price at which you take profit, and the price at which you accept you were wrong. Without a stop-loss, one bad day can undo months of gains.

## 5. Keep a record

Write down why you entered each trade and what happened. After thirty trades you will see your own patterns far more clearly than any tip can show you.

> Investments in the securities market are subject to market risks. Read all the related documents carefully before investing.
